Over the past year at the Centre for Smart Governance (CSG), I have worked at the intersection of data systems, governance, and user-centered design. My contributions span two distinct areas: building performance dashboards for digital public service platforms and conducting structured domain research in health systems and AI in GovTech.

##  Building Performance Dashboards for Digital Public Services

Government platforms generate large volumes of administrative data, but data alone does not guarantee insight. One of my core responsibilities involved designing and developing performance dashboards to enable structured monitoring of statewide digital public services.

The work began with understanding institutional workflows and administrative hierarchies. Instead of starting with charts, I began with systems:

- How does the process function end-to-end?
- What constitutes efficiency in this context?
- Which indicators meaningfully reflect performance?

From this, I helped define a structured set of KPIs aligned with operational objectives.

The dashboards were built to support:
- Cross-unit performance comparison
- Time-series trend analysis
- Drill-down views for granular inspection
- Clear visual hierarchy to reduce cognitive overload

## Health Systems Research: From Institutional Design to Measurable Outcomes

Separately, I conducted structured research on public health systems to understand how large-scale service delivery mechanisms are organized, monitored, and evaluated.

This work focused on institutional analysis rather than implementation.

### Research Scope
The study involved:
- Reviewing government reports, operational manuals, and evaluation documents
- Mapping service delivery hierarchies across administrative levels
- Understanding reporting and monitoring structures
- Identifying measurable outcomes that reflect both efficiency and service quality

A central question guided the analysis:
> What should be measured in a health system, and why?

This exercise required translating qualitative institutional design into quantifiable monitoring logic. It strengthened my ability to identify meaningful indicators within complex public systems — a skill directly applicable to performance dashboard design.

## AI in Governance: Mapping Public Sector Adoption in India

In parallel, I have been involved in a research initiative examining how Artificial Intelligence is being used across Indian central ministries and state governments.

The objective was to systematically map the landscape of AI adoption in public administration.

### Research Approach
The work involved:
- **Compiling AI initiatives** from publicly available reports, policy documents, and institutional disclosures
- **Building a structured database** of identified AI implementations
- **Classifying initiatives** based on predominant AI technique (e.g. NLP-based systems, computer vision applications, decision-support systems)
- **Categorizing use cases** across sectors such as health, agriculture, welfare, urban governance, and law enforcement

Rather than treating AI adoption as a uniform phenomenon, we analyzed patterns:
- Are systems primarily assistive, predictive, or automated?
- Is AI being deployed for efficiency gains, targeting, monitoring, or decision support?

This structured classification framework is currently being developed into a research paper/article for publication.
